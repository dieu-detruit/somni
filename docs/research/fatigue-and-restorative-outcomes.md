# Fatigue and restorative outcomes

Reviewed: 2026-11-07. Targeted narrative update, not a systematic review or an efficacy claim.

For cognitive rather than physical fatigue, see the focused [mental-fatigue audio candidates](mental-fatigue-audio-candidates.md). Motor fatigability below is an adjacent outcome, not the primary target.

## Outcome selection

Falling asleep sooner, increasing slow-wave activity (SWA), feeling less sleepy, sustaining attention, and feeling physically recovered are different outcomes. A study can improve one without improving the others. Morning restfulness and next-day fatigue are the relevant outcomes here; EEG changes are mechanistic observations, not substitutes.

## Directly relevant primary studies

### Acoustic stimulation: positive daytime findings

[Diep et al., 2021 — Acoustic enhancement of slow wave sleep on consecutive nights improves alertness and attention in chronically short sleepers](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33639484/), DOI 10.1016/j.sleep.2021.01.044. **Access: abstract.** Randomized double-blind crossover, 25 adults, two consecutive stimulation nights. The device used 50-ms tones, initially triggered by EEG slow-wave up-phase, followed by one-second intervals during N3, stopping on arousal/stage change. KSS and Samn–Perelli fatigue ratings were collected; reported improvements concerned daytime alertness/wakefulness and, after two nights, objective attention. Individual responses varied. The abstract does not establish a separate fatigue-scale effect size or bodily recovery. Device-industry affiliations warrant attention. This is not evidence for fixed monaural beats or Watch-only feedback.

### Acoustic stimulation: no additional motor recovery

[Carro-Domínguez et al., 2025; online 2024 — Overnight changes in performance fatigability and their relationship to modulated deep sleep oscillations via auditory stimulation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39420437/), DOI 10.1111/jsr.14371. **Access: abstract and figure descriptions.** Seventeen participants in the reported comparison, stimulation/sham nights, evening/morning finger tapping. Overnight tapping speed and resistance to motor slowing improved, but auditory enhancement of slow waves/spindles did not significantly add to that improvement. Individual oscillation changes did not predict motor recovery. This directly challenges treating increased SWA as proof of recovery; finger-task fatigability is still narrower than whole-body fatigue.

### Acoustic stimulation: no morning subjective improvement

[Grimaldi et al., 2019 — Strengthening sleep–autonomic interaction via acoustic enhancement of slow oscillations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7729207/), DOI 10.1093/sleep/zsz036. **Access: full-text subjective-outcome results.** Despite physiological effects, morning sleepiness did not differ between stimulation and sham (KSS 4.2 vs 4.3; p=.77), nor did subjective sleep quality (p=.83). Autonomic/EEG effects must not be presented as demonstrated relief of fatigue.

### Beats: sleep-stage latency without fatigue benefit

[Fan et al., 2024 — Binaural beats at 0.25 Hz shorten the latency to slow-wave sleep during daytime naps](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-76059-9), DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-76059-9. **Access: abstract and full-text discussion of fatigue/PVT outcomes.** Twelve healthy participants, 90-minute naps, 250-Hz carrier with different binaural conditions. Earlier sleep-stage onset did not translate into significant between-condition differences in fatigue or PVT performance. This supplies no fatigue-based reason to select 0.25 Hz, and binaural headphone delivery is different from the app's mono speaker signal.

### Sleep opportunity: a direct fatigue result outside the audio intervention

[Mantua et al., 2019 — Sleep extension reduces fatigue in healthy, normally-sleeping young adults](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31105891/), DOI 10.5935/1984-0063.20190056. **Access: abstract.** Twenty-seven adults underwent two baseline laboratory nights with eight hours in bed, then seven nights with ten. Mean sleep increased from 7.76 to 9.36 hours; subjective fatigue decreased (p=.03), while sleepiness and mood did not significantly improve. Fixed-order before/after design limits causal inference. This is evidence to track sleep opportunity, not a prescription for everyone to spend ten hours in bed.

## Interpretation for audio experiments

There is a plausible audio research route through sleep-time, EEG-informed acoustic stimulation, but the reviewed studies do not establish reliable subjective or physical recovery. Their results cannot specify an optimal mono beat frequency. Watch stage summaries cannot reproduce EEG-phase targeting.

Assess fatigue/restfulness directly and keep sleepiness separate. Interpret sleep duration, awakenings, and estimated stages as explanatory observations. Comfortable sound remains a reasonable preference; pleasantness alone does not establish next-day recovery, and increasing duration or volume is not supported by these findings.

Persistent nonrestorative sleep also warrants assessment outside an audio experiment. The [AASM diagnostic guideline for obstructive sleep apnea](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28162150/) includes unrefreshing sleep and fatigue among relevant symptoms, especially alongside snoring, gasping or witnessed breathing pauses. Symptoms and consumer sleep-stage graphs do not diagnose or exclude apnea.
