# Audio candidates reporting benefits for mental fatigue

Reviewed: 2026-11-07. Targeted search for positive primary reports, not an efficacy estimate or systematic review. Positive findings are deliberately selected here to identify hypotheses; this document must not be used to infer the overall balance of evidence.

## Scope

Mental fatigue after sustained cognitive effort is the target. Keep subjective fatigue, task performance, EEG measures, and next-morning recovery distinct. Include binaural delivery on its merits rather than restricting the search to the current mono speaker implementation. Most relevant positive studies concern waking use, not overnight recovery.

## Recovery after cognitive effort

### Axelsen, Kirk and Staiano, 2020

[On-the-Spot Binaural Beats and Mindfulness Reduces the Effect of Mental Fatigue](https://doi.org/10.1007/s41465-019-00162-3). **Access: publisher abstract and author-uploaded full-text methods/results on [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/338539597_On-the-Spot_Binaural_Beats_and_Mindfulness_Reduces_the_Effect_of_Mental_Fatigue).** Ninety participants across four groups; audio group n=23, seated-rest control n=21. Following a 90-minute AX-CPT, participants listened to a composed track for 12 minutes: left 165 Hz, right 179 Hz, giving a 14-Hz binaural beat. SART inhibitory-attention performance was preserved in the audio group while controls deteriorated. Randomization covered three groups, excluding experienced meditators. No matched track without beats isolates the binaural contribution. BRUMS fatigue was measured before intervention, not afterward: the positive result is performance after fatigue, not demonstrated relief of subjective fatigue or next-morning symptoms.

### Lim et al., 2018

[The effects on mental fatigue and the cognitive function of mechanical massage and binaural beats (brain massage) provided by massage chairs](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30057053/), DOI 10.1016/j.ctcp.2018.04.008. **Access: abstract.** Twenty-five adults, 20-minute intervention. Mechanical massage plus binaural beats reportedly reduced mental fatigue more than rest or massage alone, with improvements in several cognitive outcomes. No beats-only arm: this is a combined intervention, not evidence that audio alone reproduces the result. All authors list manufacturer R&D affiliations. Frequency, sham quality, fatigue definition and effect sizes require full-text verification before implementation.

### Music, 2025

[Music boosts the recovery of attention after mental fatigue in healthy young male subjects](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbr.2025.115539). **Access: publisher abstract and indexed methods/results excerpts.** Twenty-seven men recruited, randomized to music or rest; exclusions occurred. Five minutes of Mozart music after fatigue induction improved reaction-time performance, with a time-by-group interaction and electrophysiological changes. This is a short waking recovery intervention, not evidence of overnight restoration. The abstract labels the piece K488 while the methods name K448; resolve this reporting inconsistency before reproducing the stimulus.

## Limiting decline during cognitive effort

### Wang et al., 2022

[Listening to 15 Hz Binaural Beats Enhances the Connectivity of Functional Brain Networks in the Mental Fatigue State—An EEG Study](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci12091161). **Access: publisher-hosted [full-text PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/brainsci/brainsci-12-01161/article_deploy/brainsci-12-01161.pdf).** Sixty men randomized across four groups. During 20-minute TloadDback, stereo headphones delivered right 240/left 255 Hz (15-Hz beat), right 240/left 280 Hz (40-Hz beat), music, or bilateral 240 Hz. Authors interpret stable task accuracy and EEG-network changes as fatigue mitigation at 15 Hz. However, pure-tone accuracy also stayed stable, and fatigue ratings/PVT did not show a differential benefit. The positive conclusion is stronger than direct evidence of subjective recovery.

### Guo et al., 2015

[Effects of Relaxing Music on Mental Fatigue Induced by a Continuous Performance Task](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0136446). **Access: full-text abstract and methods/results.** Thirty-six students randomized to relaxing music or no music during a one-hour AX-CPT. The music group reported less mental fatigue afterward; control reaction times slowed while music-group times did not, with ERP differences. This directly includes subjective mental fatigue, but concerns concurrent protection during work rather than recovery during sleep; audible treatment and no active audio comparator limit attribution.

## Adjacent positive reports

- [Lane et al., 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9423966/), DOI 10.1016/S0031-9384(97)00436-8. **Abstract:** 29 participants, 30-minute vigilance task; beta beats (16/24 Hz) outperformed theta/delta (1.5/4 Hz) for detections/false alarms and negative mood. This contrast does not establish recovery versus silence or an optimal bedtime frequency.
- [ASMR, 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2025.1619424). **Full-text abstract/methods:** 28 participants, counterbalanced continuous task versus task with four-minute ASMR break. Immediate reaction-time benefit was reported, not a general behavioral effect. The no-break comparator does not isolate sound from taking a break. Some stimuli include whispering, outside the current audio scope.
- [Melnichuk et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11799511/), DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-88517-z. **Full-text abstract:** 80 participants, factorial parameter study and beat/control crossover. Some gamma/low-carrier/noise conditions improved overall attention, but not its decline over time. Attention enhancement alone is not demonstrated fatigue recovery.

## Implications

The 14-Hz post-task study supplies a concrete binaural recovery hypothesis. The 15-Hz concurrent study supplies a different, weaker fatigue-prevention hypothesis. Neither validates all-night beta stimulation. Slow sleep-oriented binaural beats and waking beta-range interventions should not be treated as interchangeable merely because both are called beats.

Reproducing binaural conditions requires independent left/right ear signals and verified stereo playback; mixing both tones into the phone speaker changes the intervention. Comfort, headphone practicality and actual playback routing need consideration if a future experiment uses this route. No app behavior changes follow automatically from this literature inventory.
